## Bug 1: the breakout detector can structurally never fire via base/pivot breakout

VERIFIED by the lead: `find_base_high()` and `find_pivot_high()` in src/screening/phase_indicators.py (~lines
189-217) compute `prices.iloc[-window:].max()` - a window that INCLUDES the current/most recent price. Since
`detect_breakout()` (phase_indicators.py ~line 837) then checks `current_price > base_high` / `current_price >
pivot_high` using that same current price as part of what `base_high`/`pivot_high` were computed from, that
comparison can mathematically never be true (current_price can be AT MOST equal to a max that includes itself,
never greater). The only breakout path that can ever actually fire is the "50 SMA Breakout" branch further down,
plus the VCP-breakout branch (which uses vcp_data, a different input). This makes the "Base Breakout"/"Pivot
Breakout" types unreachable, contradicting their comments/reasons text ("Base Breakout" never actually appears in
a real reasons list today - confirm this yourself by checking if it appears in any real historical report before
and after your fix, e.g. grep `data/daily_scans/*.txt` for "Base Breakout"/"Pivot Breakout" - it likely never has).

FIX: change both functions to exclude the current/last bar from the window used to find the resistance level -
e.g. `prices.iloc[-window-1:-1].max()` (the window immediately BEFORE today, so today's close can legitimately
exceed it) rather than `prices.iloc[-window:].max()`. Verify the exact off-by-one carefully with a test: construct
a price series where the last bar is a new high above the prior `window` bars' max, and confirm `is_buy`-eligible
breakout detection now fires where it didn't before, AND a series where the last bar is NOT a new high still
correctly does not fire. Also test the boundary (exactly equal to the prior high - decide and document whether
that counts as a breakout, and be consistent with whatever the codebase's apparent intent is elsewhere e.g. the
50-SMA breakout branch's own `>` vs `>=` choice).

## Bug 2: the fundamentals block silently exceeds its own advertised 35-point cap

VERIFIED by the lead: src/screening/signal_engine.py line ~424: `fundamental_score_final = fundamental_score *
(35 / 40)` has NO upper clamp on the raw `fundamental_score` before scaling, unlike trend_score's own pattern two
sections earlier (`trend_score_final = min(trend_score, 40) * (35 / 40)`, which DOES clamp). Raw fundamental_score
can reach revenue(15) + eps(15) + inventory(10) + margin-placeholder(10) = 50, scaled = 43.75, i.e. 8.75 points
over the documented 35-point maximum. A prior audit found this affected roughly 10.8% of the current real
fundamentals cache (2,600 files).

FIX: clamp `fundamental_score` to a maximum of 40 (its documented pre-scale raw maximum, mirroring exactly how
trend_score already does it) before the `* (35/40)` scale: `fundamental_score_final = min(fundamental_score, 40)
* (35 / 40)`. Consider (and decide, documenting your reasoning either way) whether a LOWER clamp is also needed -
the code allows `fundamental_score -= 15` as a revenue-decline penalty (line ~335), so raw fundamental_score CAN
go negative today; decide whether score_buy_signal's final `max(0, min(score, 125))` clamp already handles that
adequately at the top level, or whether the fundamentals sub-score specifically should also floor at 0 - make an
explicit, tested decision, don't leave it ambiguous.

## Bug 3: the extension-penalty is a hard step function (Antigravity's "jitter" finding)

VERIFIED by the lead: src/screening/signal_engine.py ~lines 240-253:
```python
if distance_50 > 30:
    trend_score -= 10
elif distance_50 > 20:
    trend_score -= 5
```
Two stocks at distance_50 = 19.9% and 20.1% land 5 points apart (after the 35/40 scale, 4.375 points) purely from
a fraction of a percent of price movement, with nothing in between. (NOTE: the lead independently checked the
52-week-high entry-quality proximity scoring Antigravity also flagged as a candidate cliff and found it is
ALREADY continuous/piecewise-linear at every boundary - e.g. at distance_from_52w_high=-15 both adjoining
branches evaluate to exactly 2 - do NOT "fix" that one, it isn't broken; verify this yourself before touching it,
and if you disagree with this lead assessment, say so in your report with the specific numbers rather than
silently changing it.)

FIX: replace the two-step penalty with a continuous function that matches the SAME endpoints the current steps
approximately target (0 penalty at distance_50<=20, roughly -5ish by 20-30%, -10 at distance_50>=30 and beyond) -
e.g. a linear ramp from 0 at 20% to -10 at 30%, capped at -10 beyond 30% (`-min(10, max(0, (distance_50-20)))` or
similar - choose a smooth, monotonic, clearly-reasoned formula, document the formula and why in a comment,
matching this codebase's existing style of commenting the reasoning behind each formula). The exact curve shape
is a judgment call given no historical correlation data isolates the extension penalty specifically (that's a
future SC-006 concern, not this task) - the requirement here is CONTINUITY (no jump greater than what one
percentage point of price movement would naturally cause elsewhere in the formula), not a specific "correct"
curve. Keep the boundary conditions sane: a score of exactly 0 at distance_50=20% and exactly -10 at
distance_50=30%+, changing smoothly in between, is an acceptable, defensible choice if you don't find a stronger
justification for something else.
